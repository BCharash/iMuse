Listen and classify wake events

Stop before playback. Each trigger captures 3 seconds before and 2 seconds after the detector event. Near triggers and manual marks also get clips so you can review negatives. Short clips at start, Stop or an analysis gap are flagged. The event is the detector decision, not necessarily the start of the spoken phrase.

Labels refer to the actual result: true positive = accepted wake phrase; false positive = accepted without the phrase; false negative = phrase missed or rejected; true negative = no phrase and no acceptance. A rejected candidate can be a true negative or false negative. Counts compare the same reviewed examples with the speech gate on and off.

Tap a manual marker immediately after the sound. Near-trigger clips are sampled at most once every 5 seconds; manual negative samples let you review quiet/noise even when the detector stays below that score.

Clips, labels and notes are saved with their lab recording. Deleting the recording deletes its reviews. Export a review archive for a separate copy. Playback and audio export require Stop.

Clear displayed events only hides review cards. Clear all logged data permanently removes event history, review clips, labels, notes and verification results, including saved reviews. Stop first. Full recordings, filename suggestions and downloaded exports are kept. A candidate means the wake engine fired; the speech/level gates decide whether it is accepted.

Timing: Processing ms measures work within the current audio packet. Packet → result ms includes delivery/queue wait and processing. These overlap; do not add them. Neither measures the end of your phrase to an audible response, and they exclude microphone buffering and waiting for detector windows. Manual samples without timing show —. Both measurements are included in exports.

A near trigger within one second before an actual trigger in the same recording is grouped with that trigger by default. Its audio and label stay in the export. Independent near triggers remain visible. Accepted events offer true/false positive; rejected and other non-accepted events offer true/false negative.
